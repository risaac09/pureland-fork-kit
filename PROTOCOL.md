# Facilitation protocol

This is a branch of [the journey](JOURNEY.md), taken when the work records an encounter with other people. Use this protocol for a recorded encounter between people. Pair it with the [rights and consent guide](RIGHTS-AND-CONSENT.md).

## Before recording

### Name the encounter

Write down the question the participants are living inside. State why you want to record it, who the audience is, and every use you can foresee. Name your own stake as the facilitator. Do not present the person holding the recorder as neutral.

### Hold a pre-conversation

Meet off the record. Explain the process in plain language. Ask what must stay outside the record. Agree on how a participant can pause the recording or mark something off the record during the encounter. Refusal does not count as failure, noncooperation, or a lack of reciprocity.

### Make separate consent decisions

Record a yes, no, or not-yet answer for each use:

- record audio;
- record video;
- keep the raw files for a named period;
- create a transcript;
- let named reviewers see raw material;
- share a rough cut or draft with named reviewers;
- ask for approval of each proposed public artifact;
- publish a complete work;
- publish specific clips or quotations;
- use material in research;
- process material with a named AI provider and model for a named purpose;
- use material to train or fine-tune a model;
- permit a named future use;
- give the participant a copy;
- provide a named form of compensation or return;
- rely on participant or community authority; and
- use the agreed withdrawal route, with its known limits.

Silence is not consent. Agreeing to be recorded is not agreeing to be published. Agreeing to AI transcription is not agreeing to model training. One participant cannot grant authority over knowledge, material, or decisions held by a community.

### Record custody and limits

Write down:

- who controls the raw files and backups;
- where the files live and which third parties receive them;
- the retention date and how deletion will happen;
- whether the participant receives raw and edited copies;
- who may review the material;
- how draft review works and the participant-agreed response timeframe;
- how the participant approves or refuses each actual public artifact;
- what can still be removed after publication;
- how to request withdrawal, correction, or takedown; and
- what happens if the relationship ends or a participant dies.

Use the [consent register template](templates/consent-register.md). Keep the completed register private. Do not promise deletion from copies, caches, archives, backups, model weights, or downstream uses you do not control.

## During recording

Settle in together before the recorder starts. State the consent boundary on the record. Remind participants that they can pause.

Follow the life of the encounter without treating disclosure as the measure of success. Humor and silence are allowed. If the form becomes more formal than the relationship, say so. If new sensitive material or a new use appears, stop and ask again.

## After recording

### Return before publishing

Agree with the participant on when and how you will return a note, copy, payment, or other promised form of return. Record the timeframe instead of assuming one. Return is not proof that consent exists, and publication is not a required form of return.

Show the participant every proposed public artifact for review. The participant approves the artifact itself, in the form proposed for release. A description of a future edit is not approval. Material changes create a new artifact and require a new decision.

### Respect refusal and disagreement

If a participant refuses recording, research, AI processing, publication, or return through publication, record the refusal and stop that use. Private learning may be the only permitted outcome.

When an affected person rejects the assessor's interpretation, preserve the objection in their words when possible. Distinguish disagreement with the interpretation from refusal to publish the person's material. Do not rewrite disagreement as consensus. Do not publish participant material without approval. If the artifact may still be published, offer to attach visible dissent to the public interpretation. Give the affected person a route to request correction or takedown of records the project controls.

### Handle withdrawal honestly

A participant may withdraw permission for future uses through the agreed route. Before publication, stop the refused processing and remove controlled copies whose only authority came from the withdrawn permission, within the agreed timeframe and subject to limits stated in advance. After publication, stop new controlled uses and document each retraction, correction, access restriction, or removal the project can make. Also record what the project cannot retrieve.

Withdrawal may not remove copies, archives, backups, model weights, or downstream adaptations outside the project's control. Do not claim that it can. Record the request, the decision, the actions taken, their dates, and any remaining exposure.

### Keep a decision record

Keep the minimum record needed to prove the current permissions. Keep relationship notes separate from production records. Do not keep sensitive detail just because storage is cheap.

## Aggregate learning tiers

- **Internal learning:** the material informs the practitioner's private reflection only.
- **Anonymized method learning:** an explicit opt-in permits patterns to improve the public method after a re-identification and attribution-risk review.
- **Identified public use:** permission for the specific artifact, plus the agreed payment or return.
- **Model development:** separate, explicit permission naming the provider, model, purpose, access, retention, training use, and limits on withdrawal.

## After the encounter

Run the [openness scorecard](SCORECARD.md) on the practice, not the participant. Then run the [extraction check](EXTRACTION-CHECK.md). Record the evidence and the unresolved disagreement.

This protocol does not certify a practice as safe or non-extractive. It creates decisions that another person can inspect and challenge.
