# Facilitation protocol

Use this protocol for a recorded encounter between people. Pair it with the [rights and consent guide](RIGHTS-AND-CONSENT.md).

## Before recording

### Name the encounter

Write down the question the participants are living inside. State why you want to record it, who the audience is, and every use you can foresee. Name your own stake as the facilitator. Do not present the person holding the recorder as neutral.

### Hold a pre-conversation

Meet off the record. Explain the process in plain language. Ask what must stay outside the record. Agree on how a participant can pause the recording or mark something off the record during the encounter.

### Make separate consent decisions

Record a yes, no, or not-yet answer for each use:

- record audio;
- record video;
- keep the raw files for a named period;
- create a transcript;
- share a rough cut with named reviewers;
- publish a complete work;
- publish clips or quotations;
- use material in research;
- process material with a named AI or machine-learning service;
- use material to train or fine-tune a model;
- permit a named future use.

Silence is not consent. Agreeing to be recorded is not agreeing to be published. Agreeing to an AI transcription service is not agreeing to train a model.

### Record custody and limits

Write down:

- who controls the raw files and the backups;
- where the files live and which third parties receive them;
- the retention date and how deletion will happen;
- whether the participant receives raw and edited copies;
- how review works and how long the participant has to respond;
- what can still be removed after publication;
- what happens if the relationship ends or a participant dies.

Use the [consent register template](templates/consent-register.md). Do not promise deletion from copies, caches, archives, or downstream uses you do not control.

## During recording

Settle in together before the recorder starts. State the consent boundary on the record. Remind participants that they can pause.

Follow the life of the encounter without treating disclosure as the measure of success. Humor and silence are allowed. If the form becomes more formal than the relationship, say so. If new sensitive material or a new use appears, stop and ask again.

## After recording

### Return before publishing

Within 48 hours, send a short note about what the encounter changed or clarified for you. This is a first return, not compensation, and not proof of reciprocity.

Show the participant every proposed public artifact for review. The participant approves the artifact itself. A description of a future edit is not approval.

### Handle withdrawal honestly

A participant may withdraw permission for future uses at any time. Stop new processing and remove the copies you control, unpublished, within the agreed period. For material already published or distributed, document what you can retract, what you cannot retrieve, and what notice you will give. A consent process is not honest if it promises control the project does not have.

### Keep a decision record

Keep the minimum record needed to prove the current permissions. Keep relationship notes separate from production records. Do not keep sensitive detail just because storage is cheap.

## Aggregate learning tiers

- **Internal learning:** the material informs the practitioner's private reflection only.
- **Anonymized method learning:** an explicit opt-in permits patterns to improve the public method. Describe the re-identification risk.
- **Identified public use:** permission for the specific artifact, plus a negotiated payment or return.
- **Model development:** separate, explicit permission naming the model, the purpose, the access, the retention, and the limits on withdrawal.

## After the encounter

Run the [openness scorecard](SCORECARD.md) on the practice, not the participant. Then run the [extraction check](EXTRACTION-CHECK.md). Record the evidence and the unresolved disagreement.

This protocol does not certify a practice as safe or non-extractive. It creates decisions that another person can inspect and challenge.
