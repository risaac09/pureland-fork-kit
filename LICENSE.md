## What that means, plainly

You are free to share this material and to adapt it, for any purpose, including a paid one, as long as you do two things:

1. **Credit the source.** Name this kit and link back to where you got it.
2. **Share alike.** Anything you build on this material, publish under the same license. If you fork the protocol, the scorecard, or the extraction check and change it, your version carries CC BY-SA 4.0 forward too.

The second condition is the whole point, and the reason this is not MIT or CC0. A permissive license lets anyone take material and close it back up downstream: fork it, improve it, sell the improved version under different terms, and give nothing back to the commons it came from. ShareAlike closes that door mechanically. It does not depend on anyone's goodwill. A fork that goes closed-source is a license violation, not an unlikely but permitted outcome.

## Why this license, specifically

Two earlier claims about this practice's licensing did not hold up under audit. The estate's existing public work is licensed MIT or CC0, both permissive, neither carrying a reciprocity condition, and an internal review found that gap: a practice built on the claim that value taken should be given back was shipping its own work under licenses that make no such requirement of anyone downstream. That is a real inconsistency, and this kit is the correction, not a continuation of the prior pattern.

The reciprocity precedent followed here is **Sociocracy 3.0**, whose practice guide is also licensed CC BY-SA 4.0. S3 does not state an explicit norm beyond what ShareAlike itself requires; its reciprocity is the license mechanism plus an open invitation to contribute and translate, not a separate written pledge layered on top. This kit follows that same shape rather than claiming a stronger commitment than the precedent it is drawing on actually makes.

CC BY-NC-SA, the noncommercial variant InsightShare uses for the toolkit this kit builds on, was considered and set aside. A noncommercial clause would block paid facilitation work built on this method, and this practice's economic reality includes paid facilitation. ShareAlike without the noncommercial restriction keeps the reciprocity requirement while leaving the door open for someone to build a livelihood on this material, provided what they build stays open in turn.

## The written reciprocity expectation

Beyond the license terms themselves, one expectation, stated plainly rather than left implicit:

If you fork this kit and run it, on your own practice or someone else's, send back what you find. Not the artifacts you produce with it (those are the participants' and yours, and the protocol governs their consent separately) but what the instruments taught you: where the scorecard's four accesses surprised you, where the extraction check found a real number instead of "unmeasurable," where the protocol's steps didn't fit your situation and what you changed. A reply that disagrees with this kit is worth exactly as much as a reply that confirms it. Both are contact. See [README.md](README.md) for where to send it.

This expectation is not enforceable the way the license is. It is a request from one practice to another, made honestly, without pretending it carries the force of law. The license protects the commons. The request is what makes it a relationship instead of just a legal state.
