# Provenance

*(Pure)Land fork kit, component 5 of 5. What this kit builds on, what it has not built yet, and where every claim in it can be checked.*

## The precedent this builds on

This kit is not the first attempt to fuse open method, documentary practice, and built-in measurement. **InsightShare's Participatory Video and the Most Significant Change toolkit** already does that, earlier and at larger scale:

> Sara Asadullah and Soledad Muñiz, *Participatory Video and the Most Significant Change: A Guide for Facilitators*, InsightShare, 2015, produced in partnership with UNICEF. Licensed CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. `insightshare.org/resources/participatory-video-and-the-most-significant-change/`

PVMSC pairs a participatory-video method with the Most Significant Change technique, a structured way of collecting and selecting the stories participants themselves judge most significant, so that evaluation runs on participant-authored narrative rather than externally imposed metrics. It has been used across UNICEF programming internationally.

This kit differs in what it measures, not in whether it opens: PVMSC's measurement layer is donor-facing program monitoring and evaluation. This kit's scorecard and extraction check are reflexive, built to be run on the practice's own openness and the practice's own value flows, not on a program's outcomes for a funder. That difference is real but narrow, and it does not make this kit first. It makes it second, building on ground InsightShare already opened. Cite them before you cite this.

## What this kit does not yet have

Named plainly, so a fork inherits an accurate picture rather than an inflated one:

- **No validation.** The scorecard and extraction check are specified instruments. They have not been run against a second, independent case to confirm they measure what they claim to measure.
- **No mapping to any other codebook.** This kit does not claim compatibility with, or grounding in, any other measurement framework beyond what is cited here.
- **No computed extraction coefficient from real data.** The worked example in this kit is synthetic. No real practice's C_r, A_r, V_r has been scored and published using this instrument as of this kit's release.
- **One informal baseline, not a study.** The four-access framing behind the scorecard was run once, informally, against the estate that produced this kit. That single run is not evidence the instrument generalizes. Treat every number in the scorecard as a hypothesis about your own practice until you have run it more than once.

## Sources, checked against primary material

**Herbert A. Simon**, "Designing Organizations for an Information-Rich World," in Martin Greenberger, ed., *Computers, Communications, and the Public Interest*, Johns Hopkins Press, 1971, pp. 37 to 52. Source of "a wealth of information creates a poverty of attention," the founding statement of attention as the scarce resource an information architecture competes for.

**Nick Couldry and Ulises A. Mejias**, *The Costs of Connection: How Data Is Colonizing Human Life and Appropriating It for Capitalism*, Stanford University Press, 2019. ISBN 978-1503609747. Source of "data colonialism": the appropriation of human life so that data can be continuously extracted from it.

**Matthew A. Killingsworth and Daniel T. Gilbert**, "A Wandering Mind Is an Unhappy Mind," *Science* 330(6006):932, 2010. DOI 10.1126/science.1192439. Roughly 47 percent of sampled waking moments involve a mind on something other than what it is doing, and wandering itself predicts lower reported happiness independent of the activity.

**Carlo Rovelli**, *Helgoland: Making Sense of the Quantum Revolution*, trans. Erica Segre and Simon Carnell, Riverhead Books, 2021. Contains a chapter on Nagarjuna and Madhyamaka emptiness in relation to relational quantum mechanics. Rovelli's own position, stated plainly: Nagarjuna does not prove relational QM. Madhyamaka offers a conceptual space in which relational QM stops looking paradoxical. That is the relationship this kit holds too: philosophy licenses the interpretation, physics licenses the geometry, and neither should be made to do the other's job.

**Stephanie Russo Carroll, Ibrahim Garba, Oscar L. Figueroa-Rodríguez, Jarita Holbrook, et al.**, "The CARE Principles for Indigenous Data Governance," *Data Science Journal* 19(1), 2020. **James J. Gibson**, *The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception*, Houghton Mifflin, 1979. Source of "affordance": what an environment offers an organism in relation, not a property of either side alone. The term this whole practice's atom is built from.

## Pali sources for the five-vow framing referenced in this kit's companion writing

Checked against SuttaCentral's Pali text, not against a secondary paraphrase.

- The undertaking formula ("*veramaṇisikkhāpadaṁ samādiyāmi*, I undertake the training rule of abstaining from...") is carried by **Khuddakapāṭha 2** (the ten training rules). The specific five-precept wording most people know, including "*kāmesumicchācāraṁ pahāya*" for the third precept, matches **Aṅguttara Nikāya 8.39**. The lay five precepts as commonly recited are a composite of these two texts, not a single sutta.
- The fifth precept's full Pali, "*surāmerayamajjapamādaṭṭhānā veramaṇisikkhāpadaṁ samādiyāmi*," names its object precisely: not intoxicants as such, but *pamādaṭṭhāna*, an occasion or basis for heedlessness. Confirmed at Khp 2 and AN 8.39.
- **Majjhima Nikāya 56** (the Upāli Sutta) is the canonical source for ranking action by body, speech, and mind (*kāya-, vacī-, mano-kamma*), against a Jain interlocutor's ranking by three "rods." The Buddha states three times that mental action is the most consequential of the three.
- "*Indriyesu guttadvāro*," the doors of the senses guarded, is stock canonical phrasing, confirmed at **Dīgha Nikāya 2**.
- The three doors of action (*kāya-, vacī-, mano-dvāra*) and the mind-door cognitive process (*mano-dvāra-vīthi*) are Abhidhamma vocabulary, confirmed in the **Abhidhammattha-saṅgaha**, chapters V and IV respectively, not sutta-level terminology. The three-fold classification they formalize is sutta-level (MN 56); the door language that names it precisely is commentarial.
- The claim that the fifth precept's breach opens toward breaking the other four is well attested in secondary scholarship, **Peter Harvey, *An Introduction to Buddhist Ethics*, Cambridge University Press, 2000, pp. 77 to 79**, but no single primary Pali passage stating it in those exact terms was located. Hold it as the tradition's settled reading, not as a direct quotation.
- The Buddha's last words, "*vayadhammā saṅkhārā appamādena sampādethā*," conditions fall apart, persist with diligence, are confirmed at **Dīgha Nikāya 16**.
