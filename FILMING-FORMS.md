# Filming forms: self, dyadic, triadic, environmental

This is a branch of [the journey](JOURNEY.md), taken alongside the [facilitation protocol](PROTOCOL.md) whenever the recording device is a camera. The protocol governs any recorded encounter. This document names four forms a camera-based encounter can take, and reads each one for what it can witness and what it risks taking instead.

## The claim

A camera does not automatically take from the person in front of it. It can also witness: hold a moment open, without narrowing the person into a soundbite or a piece of content. Which one happens is set by the form the filming takes and how that form is held, not by the footage's beauty, the subject's willingness on the day, or the maker's intent.

This taxonomy is PureLand's own, drawn from practice, not an established film-theory or ethnographic category system. Treat it the way the rest of the toolbox treats its own instruments: a hypothesis about where extraction enters camera-based work, open to correction by anyone it's used on.

## The four forms

Each form names who is in front of the camera, who holds it, and what relational structure that creates. A single project can move through more than one form; name the form in use at any given time, not the project as a whole.

### Self-filming

One person, camera turned on themselves. No second party present in the frame or behind the lens.

- **What it can witness:** unrehearsed contact with oneself, without a second person's presence changing what gets said. A person can reach something alone that a witness would have foreclosed.
- **What it risks taking:** performance for an imagined audience replacing the encounter with itself. The self becomes a soundbite extracted from the same person who produced it, with no one else to notice the substitution happening.
- **Question before rolling:** is this footage of an encounter with myself, or is it already footage of an audience I'm imagining?
- **Consent beyond the protocol's baseline:** none from another party. The open question is internal: what use, retention, and publication does the person filming themselves actually want, separate from what feels available in the moment.

### Dyadic filming

Two people. One holds the camera, or a camera runs unattended between them.

- **What it can witness:** a conversation that could not happen with a third party or a spectator present. A moment where someone says a thing they could not otherwise say, because the room stayed small.
- **What it risks taking:** the camera holder has a stake and an asymmetric hand on the edit. The other person becomes subject to a story they did not shape, even when they said yes to being filmed.
- **Question before rolling:** whose telling does this become, once it's cut, and does the other person know that yet?
- **Consent beyond the protocol's baseline:** the held party's separate sign-off on edit authority, distinct from consent to record. Agreeing to be filmed in a dyad is not agreeing to the other person's cut.

### Triadic filming

Two participants and a camera holder present as a third relational position, not a neutral observer.

- **What it can witness:** material that needs a witness distinct from the two speakers, such as repair, mediation, or something one party wants seen by someone besides the other. The third position can hold a moment two people alone could not hold for each other.
- **What it risks taking:** the third position claiming a neutrality it does not have, or the footage turning into two people being spoken about rather than two people speaking. A two-against-one frame can form without anyone intending it.
- **Question before rolling:** what is the camera holder's stake in how this reads, and have both participants heard it stated?
- **Consent beyond the protocol's baseline:** both participants' separate confirmation that the third position's stake has been named out loud, before recording starts, not discovered later in the footage.

### Environmental filming

A place, a landscape, a room. People absent from frame, or present only peripherally.

- **What it can witness:** context, the field a person's contact happens inside. A place can carry memory, use, and beauty that names something true about the people connected to it without asking any one of them to perform on camera.
- **What it risks taking:** the place standing in for the people who live in or from it, without their consent, because no one on camera was there to ask. Extracting the setting of someone's life as content is still extraction, even with no face in the shot.
- **Question before rolling:** who is connected to this place who is not being asked, and does this footage speak for them or past them?
- **Consent beyond the protocol's baseline:** where the place is identifiably someone's home, land, workplace, or community site, that person or community's consent, even though they are not the visible subject.

## Running the extraction check per form

The [extraction check](EXTRACTION-CHECK.md)'s value-trace table asks what leaves a person and where it accumulates. That answer changes by form:

| Form | Who value can leave | Where it risks accumulating |
|---|---|---|
| Self-filming | The person filming themselves | An imagined or actual audience the person never fully consented to serve |
| Dyadic | Whichever party did not hold the camera | The camera holder's authorship and archive |
| Triadic | Both participants | The third position's framing, if it publishes without both participants' review |
| Environmental | People connected to the place, though absent from frame | Whoever holds and distributes the footage |

Run the check once per form in use, not once for the whole project. A project that moves from environmental establishing shots into a dyadic conversation has two value traces to trace, not one.

## What this does not do

Naming a form is not a safety guarantee. Self-filming can still extract; environmental filming can still take from an absent community; a well-run triad can still misread its own neutrality. The [facilitation protocol](PROTOCOL.md)'s separate consent decisions, the [rights and consent guide](RIGHTS-AND-CONSENT.md), and the practitioner's own read using the [practice frame](PRACTICE-FRAME.md) still govern what actually happens. This document only names which relational structure is in play, so the questions above get asked in the form they actually need.

This taxonomy has not been field-tested. No trial has checked whether these four forms are the right cut, whether a fifth form is missing, or whether the risks named for each hold up against real footage. Treat a disagreement about the categories themselves as valid evidence against the taxonomy, not as a misunderstanding of it.
