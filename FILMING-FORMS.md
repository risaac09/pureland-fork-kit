# Filming forms: self, dyadic, triadic, environmental

This is a branch of [the journey](JOURNEY.md), taken alongside the [facilitation protocol](PROTOCOL.md) whenever the recording device is a camera. The protocol governs any recorded encounter. This document names four forms a camera-based encounter can take, reads each one for what it can witness and what it risks taking instead, and asks separately who is holding the camera.

## The claim

A camera does not automatically take from the person in front of it. It can also witness: hold a moment open, without narrowing the person into a soundbite or a piece of content. Which one happens is set by the form the filming takes and how that form is held, not by the footage's beauty, the subject's willingness on the day, or the maker's intent.

Holding is built before the camera exists, in the pre-conversation the [protocol](PROTOCOL.md#hold-a-pre-conversation) already asks for. A camera switched on inside a container nobody built cannot be held into one afterward.

This taxonomy is PureLand's own, drawn from practice, not an established film-theory or ethnographic category system. So are the holder axis, the editing interval, and the return default below. Treat them the way the rest of the toolbox treats its own instruments: hypotheses about where extraction enters camera-based work, open to correction by anyone they are used on.

## The four forms

Each form names who is in the relation and what structure that creates. It does not name whose hand is on the camera. That is a second axis, and the two come apart, so read the forms alongside "Who holds the camera" below. A single project can move through more than one form; name the form in use at any given time, not the project as a whole.

### Self-filming

One person in the encounter. No second person operates the camera or appears in frame. Where a second party set the camera up, briefed it, or will edit what it records, that party's stake and edit authority need stating before the camera changes hands, and the encounter is not self-filming in the sense used here.

- **What it can witness:** unrehearsed contact with oneself, without a second person's presence changing what gets said. A person can reach something alone that a witness would have foreclosed.
- **What it risks taking:** performance for an imagined audience replacing the encounter with itself. The self becomes a soundbite extracted from the same person who produced it, with no one else to notice the substitution happening.
- **Question before rolling:** what is being watched, scored, or waited on while this runs, and by whom? A person filming alone in a room can still be performing for a measurement they agreed to weeks earlier.
- **Consent beyond the protocol's baseline:** none from another party, when no other party has a stake. The open question is internal: what use, retention, and publication does the person filming themselves actually want, separate from what feels available in the moment. Where another party set the camera up or will cut the material, the dyadic form's edit-authority sign-off applies instead.

### Dyadic filming

Two people in the encounter.

- **What it can witness:** a conversation that could not happen with a third party or a spectator present. A moment where someone says a thing they could not otherwise say, because the room stayed small.
- **What it risks taking:** whoever holds the edit has a stake and an asymmetric hand on how this reads. The other person becomes subject to a story they did not shape, even when they said yes to being filmed.
- **Question before rolling:** whose telling does this become, once it is cut, and does the other person know that yet?
- **Consent beyond the protocol's baseline:** the held party's separate sign-off on edit authority, distinct from consent to record. Agreeing to be filmed in a dyad is not agreeing to the other person's cut.

### Triadic filming

Two participants and a third person present as a relational position, not a neutral observer.

- **What it can witness:** material that needs a witness distinct from the two speakers, such as repair, mediation, or something one party wants seen by someone besides the other. The third position can hold a moment two people alone could not hold for each other.
- **What it risks taking:** the third position claiming a neutrality it does not have, or the footage turning into two people being spoken about rather than two people speaking. A two-against-one frame can form without anyone intending it.
- **Question before rolling:** what is the third position's stake in how this reads, and have both participants heard it stated?
- **Consent beyond the protocol's baseline:** both participants' separate confirmation that the third position's stake has been named out loud, before recording starts, not discovered later in the footage.

### Environmental filming

A place, a landscape, a room. People absent from frame, or present only peripherally.

- **What it can witness:** context, the field a person's contact happens inside. A place can carry memory, use, and beauty that names something true about the people connected to it without asking any one of them to perform on camera.
- **What it risks taking:** the place standing in for the people who live in or from it, without their consent, because no one on camera was there to ask. Extracting the setting of someone's life as content is still extraction, even with no face in the shot.
- **Question before rolling:** who is connected to this place who is not being asked, and does this footage speak for them or past them?
- **Consent beyond the protocol's baseline:** the [protocol's community-authority rule](PROTOCOL.md#make-separate-consent-decisions), at full strength. If the necessary community authority is absent or disputed, do not collect, process, or publish. An empty frame does not soften that rule. This is the form where the affected people are least able to object, because they are not in the room.

## Who holds the camera

The four forms count the relation. The holder is a separate question, asked per moment rather than per project:

- Who holds the camera now, and who set it up?
- Did it change hands, and in which direction along the power gradient?
- If it runs unattended, who placed and framed it, and who can stop it?
- Who holds the edit hand, whether or not they held the camera?

Read the holder off the headcount and the two come apart in the case that matters most. A facilitator builds an encounter over weeks, hands the camera to the participant, leaves the room, and makes the cut afterward. The moment satisfies the self-filming definition exactly. The encounter does not, because the party holding the edit hand is absent from the room and fully present in the relation. Reading it as self-filming lands on "no consent needed from another party," which is wrong.

A handoff down the power gradient removes the operator's framing from the moment, and the operator's read on when to keep rolling. It does not remove the operator's stake, because the stake returns at the edit. Handing over the camera while keeping all edit authority is a real move and a partial one. Calling the result participant-led overstates it.

## Wait before editing

The [protocol's editing interval](PROTOCOL.md#wait-before-editing) governs the window after any recorder stops. For footage, the window covers assembly as well as the cut. No selects pass, and nothing shown to a colleague for a read. Footage invites that early pass more than audio does, because sorting it looks like work.

## Running the extraction check per form

The [extraction check](EXTRACTION-CHECK.md)'s value-trace table asks what leaves a person and where it accumulates. Naming an accumulation site does not empty it, so the fourth column names what emptying looks like.

| Form | Who value can leave | Where it risks accumulating | What emptying it looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| Self-filming | The person filming themselves | An imagined or actual audience the person never fully consented to serve | The person keeps the files and sets the audience later, off camera. Any other party's copy is named, dated, and justified in the decision record, or it is not kept |
| Dyadic | Whichever party did not hold the edit | The camera holder's authorship and archive | Raw and edited material goes back to the other party. The maker keeps nothing by default and justifies each retained item |
| Triadic | Both participants | The third position's framing, if it publishes without both participants' review | Both participants receive the raw material, not only the cut. Each retained item needs both, separately |
| Environmental | People connected to the place, though absent from frame | Whoever holds and distributes the footage | Material returns to the authority that permitted it, under the same non-retention default. Where publication was refused, no archived copy is held against a later ask |

Default to returning raw and edited material, not only the finished artifact. A participant who receives a short cut and none of the footage behind it has received the maker's reading of the encounter rather than the encounter.

Hold this short of an absolute. The [protocol](PROTOCOL.md#handle-withdrawal-honestly) is right that no maker can promise removal from copies, backups, and downstream uses they do not control. Return and non-retention are a default a maker sets and can be held to. Deletion elsewhere is a claim a maker cannot back. Say both, in those terms, before recording.

Run the check once per form in use, not once for the whole project. A project that moves from environmental establishing shots into a dyadic conversation has two value traces to trace, not one. Run it after the encounter, never inside it. Scoring an encounter while it happens has changed what the encounter is, and the person on camera can feel the scoring even when nobody says a number out loud.

## What this does not do

Naming a form is not a safety guarantee. Self-filming can still extract; environmental filming can still take from an absent community; a well-run triad can still misread its own neutrality. The [facilitation protocol](PROTOCOL.md)'s separate consent decisions, the [rights and consent guide](RIGHTS-AND-CONSENT.md), and the practitioner's own read using the [practice frame](PRACTICE-FRAME.md) still govern what actually happens. This document only names which relational structure is in play, and who is holding what, so the questions above get asked in the form they actually need.

None of this has been field-tested. No trial has checked whether these four forms are the right cut, whether a fifth is missing, whether separating the holder from the headcount catches anything a practitioner would otherwise miss, or whether the risks named for each form hold up against real footage. Treat a disagreement about the categories themselves as valid evidence against the taxonomy, not as a misunderstanding of it. See [RESEARCH-STATUS.md](RESEARCH-STATUS.md) for the claims and what would count against them.

Where the newer material comes from is a weaker record still. The editing interval and the return default come from one filmmaking practice this kit draws on, used across a small number of engagements, not from a trial. That practice's own written self-assessment reports its per-encounter checks getting skipped under deadline more often than they run. This document asks more than its source practice reliably does, which is the ordinary condition of an instrument like this one. The gap is a reason to test the document, not a reason to trust it.
