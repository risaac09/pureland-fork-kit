Can a way of handling information give you more control over your own attention without also making you easier to exploit?

That is the question PureLand tests, not a claim it has already answered. It combines contemplative self-observation, information-practice analysis, bounded adaptation, and accountable return.

  • give people more control over their attention
  • give people more useful access without exposing them or making them easier to mine
  • build permission, credit, and fair return into how information gets handled
  • keep the practice open to challenge, so the people it affects can question it, change it, or refuse it
  • give reusable methods and rights-cleared research back to a shared commons

What it studies

Evaluation without attention or relationship can become another extraction apparatus. Contemplative and somatic practice without evidence can become private conviction. A map of information movement without consent, attribution, and return can ignore who bears its cost.

PureLand is a synthesis informed by Buddhist practice, evaluation, and somatic practice. The project's mappings are original synthesis. They are not Buddhist doctrine, clinical categories, or validated measures.

Open source is the release phase of the practice, not the whole practice.

PureLand defines generosity as increasing another person's ability to understand, adapt, contest, or refuse. The Buddhist sources do not establish that project definition. Participant material, protected knowledge, and relationship records may need to remain private.

The project name establishes no lineage relationship to Pure Land Buddhist traditions. The mark diagrams the papañca causal sequence PRACTICE-FRAME.md cites; it replaced an earlier adapted ensō, retired along with the Zen association it carried.

adaptable

“Accepting and investigating the reality of change.” One of the four access questions PureLand reads a practice through: is it understandable, reachable, adaptable, traceable?

The method

The method walks in six ordered stations, from naming the practice to returning what was learned. Recording is optional. The first pilot does not require participant recordings.

I
Ground

Name the practice, the boundary, and the people it affects. Write one result that would count against the method.

II
Observe

Run the practice frame. Notice what's true in you before assigning it to the system.

III
Map

Read the four access questions with the openness scorecard.

IV
Trace

Run the extraction check. Trace what leaves, where it accumulates, and what returns.

V
Adapt

Choose one bounded change. Watch the cost and the result.

VI
Return

Write up what happened and submit it as a field report.

precedent

One identified precedent for combining participatory media and evaluation is InsightShare's Participatory Video and the Most Significant Change. PureLand does not claim to originate that combination.

The toolbox travels both ways

A fork is judged by both directions of travel. Code and method can leave the origin. Something, learning, credit, correction, a returned report, has to travel back for the fork to be more than extraction with a license file attached.

leaves the origin

Walk the journey, the method's one curated path. Fork the instruments. Open a public-safe Stage 1 scoping issue. Document a failure or refusal. Propose a correction through the contribution process.

returns

A cleared public report when permitted, or private learning, refusal, unmeasurable, correction, disagreement, or method revision. Public reports enter the field-trial ledger; other returns remain within their rights boundary.

Current result and what remains untested

FT-001 is a maintainer-side, AI-assisted partial dry run on Alchemy. No human performed Observe. No second reader or affected user challenged the reading. The assessor chose the seven-surface boundary. The assessor interpreted some analysis as additional, but the record cannot separate the journey's effect from familiarity, time spent, or the extraction check alone.

Alchemy uses a local-first technical design intended to reduce extraction. FT-001 did not observe a user outcome. It found that traceability conflicts with intentional deletion and left one executed adaptation inside an open follow-up window.

The instruments still have no independent field result, reliability estimate, validity result, threshold, certification, causal result, or attention-sovereignty outcome. Proposed sample and context targets remain planning choices, not derived evidence gates.

Isaac Rubinstein maintains the project and holds merge and release authority. That concentration is a project risk, not a mature commons.

correction, recorded

The unvalidated extraction coefficient and composite thresholds were retired. Missing evidence stays unmeasurable; there is no composite score.

Where this leaves you
