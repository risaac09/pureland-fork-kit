# Rights and consent

This guide separates repository licensing, participant permission, community authority, and the decision to publish a specific artifact. None substitutes for the others.

## Repository license

The repository license grants reuse rights only for material the licensor has authority to license. For copies already received under its terms, the project may not be able to withdraw those permissions.

## Participant permission

Consent governs the collection, processing, retention, and publication of participant material. Record a separate decision for every use. Consent does not become an open license unless the rights holder deliberately grants one.

A participant approves the actual artifact proposed for release, not a description of an edit that does not yet exist. A material change requires another decision. A participant may veto publication of their own material under this project's rules even when the maintainer believes the interpretation is fair.

## Community and third-party authority

One participant cannot grant rights over protected community knowledge or settle a decision held by a community. Record who may decide under the relevant community protocol. If that authority is unknown, disputed, or unavailable, mark the proposed use `not yet` and keep it private.

Copyright permission does not settle confidentiality, privacy, publicity, data protection, contractual limits, or community authority. A public source is not automatically cleared for every reuse.

## Two-stage field-test intake

### Stage 1: public-safe scoping

A public issue may state:

- the practice in general terms;
- why the question matters;
- the proposed scope;
- whether participant, client, confidential, community-held, or identifying material exists;
- what categories must remain private; and
- one condition that would count against the method.

Do not include names, excerpts, recordings, records, protected knowledge, or evidence links that expose participant or protected material. Public evidence links are not required when protected material may be involved. Opening an issue scopes a possible field test. It does not mean that the project accepted the proposal or approved any use.

### Stage 2: private rights review

Stage 2 begins only after the maintainer establishes an appropriate private review channel and confirms the rights basis for the proposed review. The project does not name a default private system here. Until the channel and rights basis are resolved, no participant or protected material should enter a public issue, pull request, repository record, or AI system.

The private review identifies who may authorize collection, review, processing, and each proposed publication. It records refusals and unresolved authority as `no` or `not yet`. Review does not create permission. The people who hold the relevant rights make the decisions.

## What `public-safe` means

Material is public-safe only when a documented review concludes that the exact material and proposed artifact can be released under current authority, with the minimum necessary exposure. The review must address:

- participant material;
- identifying and indirectly identifying information;
- confidential and client records;
- protected community knowledge;
- third-party copyright;
- privacy and publicity interests;
- artifact-specific publication permission;
- re-identification risk, including combinations of details;
- attribution risk, including unwanted credit or implied endorsement;
- AI processing;
- model training or fine-tuning;
- retention and withdrawal limits;
- the reviewer's identity or role;
- the decision date; and
- the next review date or the reason no further review is planned.

Record the decision, the authority relied on, the conditions, and the unresolved risks. Use a role instead of a name in a public record when naming the reviewer would create exposure. Keep the underlying consent register and protected evidence private. A Boolean checkbox alone is not a rights review. Unknown authority means the material is not public-safe.

## Refusal, disagreement, correction, and takedown

Refusal does not count as failure, noncooperation, or lack of reciprocity. No public field report is required. Private learning can be the complete outcome.

When an affected person rejects an assessor's interpretation:

1. Preserve the objection without rewriting it as agreement.
2. Record whether the person objects to the interpretation, refuses publication of their material, or both.
3. Do not publish participant material without artifact-specific approval.
4. When publication is permitted, offer to attach the person's visible dissent to the public interpretation.
5. Provide a correction or takedown route for records the project controls.

The maintainer records the request, response, action, decision owner, decision date, and review date. A rights dispute may use the independent-review route in [GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md).

## Withdrawal

Withdrawal can stop future uses that the project controls. Before publication, follow the agreed route and timeframe, and remove controlled copies whose only authority came from the withdrawn permission. After publication, stop new controlled uses and record any correction, takedown, access restriction, or notice the project completes.

The project cannot promise deletion from lawful copies already distributed, public archives, backups or caches outside its control, existing model weights, or downstream adaptations. State known limits before collecting material. Record the request, every action taken, what remains, and why.

## Minimum operating rules

- Keep participant material and completed consent registers out of the public repository.
- Collect only what the stated purpose requires.
- Name every third-party processor and its retention terms.
- Get separate permission for AI processing and for model development.
- Set a deletion or review date before collecting.
- Keep a protected record of current permissions without retaining unnecessary disclosures.
- Recheck authority when the artifact, audience, processor, purpose, or retention period changes.
- Take legal questions to qualified counsel in the relevant jurisdiction.

Use [templates/consent-register.md](templates/consent-register.md) as a private decision record, not as universal legal language.
