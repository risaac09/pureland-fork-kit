# Rights and consent

This guide separates four things that open projects often blur together.

## Repository license

The [repository license](PROVENANCE.md#open-and-ai-system-references) grants reuse rights only for material the licensor actually has the authority to license. For copies already received under its terms, it cannot be taken back.

## Participant permission

Consent governs the collection, processing, retention, and publication of participant material. It must name the specific use. It does not become an open license unless the rights holder deliberately grants one.

## Withdrawal

Withdrawal can stop future uses that the project controls. It may not retrieve lawful copies already distributed, public archives, model weights, backups outside the project's control, or downstream adaptations. State these limits before collecting anything. Record the steps actually taken after a withdrawal.

## Privacy and confidentiality

Copyright permission does not settle privacy, confidentiality, publicity, data protection, or community authority. An open license is not a privacy waiver.

## Two-stage intake

### Stage 1: public-safe scoping

A public issue may describe only:

- the practice in general terms;
- why the question matters;
- the proposed scope;
- whether participant, client, confidential, community-held, or identifying material exists;
- what must remain private; and
- one disconfirming condition.

Do not place evidence links, excerpts, names, indirect identifiers, consent records, or protected material in a public issue. When participant or protected material may be involved, Stage 1 does not require evidence links.

### Stage 2: private rights review

Stage 2 begins only after the maintainer establishes an appropriate private review channel and confirms who has authority for the proposed collection, review, processing, and publication. The repository does not designate a private channel by default. If no appropriate channel is available, pause the intake. No participant or protected material should enter a public issue while the channel or rights basis remains unresolved.

Stage 2 is a rights review, not an acceptance of the proposal or a promise that any work or publication will follow.

## Public-safe determination

`Public-safe` means a reviewer has documented why a specific record or artifact can be made public without exceeding the permissions and authority that apply to it. Anonymity alone does not make material public-safe. Removing direct names does not resolve indirect identification, community authority, confidentiality, copyright, publicity, attribution, or downstream-use risk.

For each proposed public artifact, keep a private review record with a status such as clear, blocked, or not yet clear; the basis for that status; relevant limits; and any action required. The record must cover:

- participant material;
- direct and indirect identifying information;
- confidential and client records;
- protected or community-held knowledge, including who can authorize its use;
- third-party copyright and other reuse restrictions;
- privacy and publicity interests;
- artifact-specific publication permission for the actual version;
- re-identification risk, including combinations of otherwise ordinary details;
- attribution risk, including unwanted credit, omission, or exposure through credit;
- AI processing, with the provider, model, purpose, inputs, retention, and access named;
- model training or fine-tuning as a separate decision;
- retention terms and the known limits of withdrawal;
- the reviewer's identity or accountable role;
- the decision date; and
- the next review date or the event that requires another review.

A Boolean checkbox alone is not a rights review. A new artifact, material edit, new audience, new processor, or new use requires a fresh determination.

## Participant authority and disagreement

Each affected person controls permission for that person's own material. One collaborator or participant cannot grant authority for another person's material or for community-held knowledge unless the relevant authority is established.

A participant approves the actual artifact and version, not a description of a future artifact. Refusal is not failure, noncooperation, or lack of reciprocity.

When an affected person rejects an assessor's interpretation:

1. Preserve the objection in the private review record.
2. Record separately whether the person objects to publication, disagrees with the interpretation, or both.
3. Do not publish that person's material without artifact-specific approval.
4. When publication is permitted, invite the person to provide or approve the wording of dissent attached to the public interpretation. Without approved wording, state only that the interpretation remains disputed when that statement is public-safe.
5. Do not rewrite disagreement as consensus.
6. Provide a correction or takedown route for records the project controls, using [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) when the request contains protected information.

Disputed rights or closure decisions follow the independent-review process in [GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md) when feasible.

## Minimum operating rules

- Keep participant material out of the public repository.
- Collect only what the stated purpose requires.
- Name every third-party processor and its retention terms.
- Get separate permission for AI processing and for model development.
- Set a deletion or review date before collecting.
- Keep a protected record of current permissions without keeping unnecessary disclosures.
- Record refusals, changes, withdrawal requests, and the actions taken without turning the record into a public disclosure.
- Take legal questions to qualified counsel in the relevant jurisdiction.

Use [templates/consent-register.md](templates/consent-register.md) as a decision record, not as universal legal language.
