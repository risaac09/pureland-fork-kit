# PureLand

PureLand is the applied expression of the thesis, method, toolbox, and hypothesis. It is where a person carries one real information practice from question to return without mistaking the interface or process for evidence.

## Current form

Two routes put PureLand into use:

- [the journey](JOURNEY.md), the canonical six-station route through the method; and
- [Alchemy](https://alchemy.rubinsteinproductions.com), an optional local-first companion for a person's own information intake.

Alchemy does not become the PureLand method or a validated result. Its diagnostic is a prompt for Ground. Its capture loop supports Observe. The app itself can be assessed at Map and Trace.

## Design requirements

Any PureLand interface should:

- begin with one bounded practice, not a general personality diagnosis;
- ask for a disconfirming result before it asks for a score;
- make stopping and leaving visible;
- keep the four access readings and three reciprocity bands separate;
- show missing evidence as `unmeasurable`;
- expose the cost, disagreement, and possible new harm of an adaptation;
- require separate permission for recording, publication, research, and AI use;
- end with return, refusal, or an explicit reason no public return is permitted;
- collect no participant material by default.

## Input and output

**Input:** one information practice, its boundary, affected people, available evidence, permission, and a result that could count against the method.

**Output:** one bounded adaptation and a rights-cleared record of evidence, burden, disagreement, result, and return. A private or withheld record can be the correct output.

## Boundary

PureLand connects the [method](METHOD.md), [toolbox](TOOLBOX.md), and evidence cycle. It produces no composite index, certification, or promise of attention sovereignty. The method sets a reason to collect less when more collection would increase exposure without improving the inquiry.

## Test before expansion

The next test is whether a person outside the project can complete the journey, preserve the required boundaries, and produce a record another reader can inspect. If an interface creates confusion without improving completion or evidence quality, it should change or be removed.
