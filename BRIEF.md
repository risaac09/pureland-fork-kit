# Pure(Land): a brief for testing the method

PureLand shares the whole practice because no part can keep itself honest alone, and because work built from other people's teachings, attention, and expression owes back more than an output.

## What are we trying to change?

PureLand asks whether an information practice can increase attention sovereignty without increasing exposure or extractability. Its goals are to:

- increase attention sovereignty;
- increase useful access without increasing exposure or extractability;
- build consent, attribution, and value return into information practices;
- make the practice contestable, so affected people can question, change, or refuse it; and
- return reusable methods and rights-cleared research to a commons.

Evaluation without attention or relationship can become another extraction apparatus. Contemplative and somatic practice without evidence can become private conviction. Information theory without consent, attribution, and return can describe movement while ignoring who bears its cost.

PureLand is a synthesis informed by Buddhist practice, information theory, evaluation, and somatic practice. It does not claim that Buddhist categories are technical taxonomies or that Shannon's theory explains meaning or ethics.

## Why share the whole practice?

Open source is the release phase of the practice, not the whole practice. Generosity means increasing another person's ability to understand, adapt, contest, or refuse. It does not mean publishing everything. Participant material, protected knowledge, and relationship records may need to remain private.

The whole-practice thesis therefore joins diagnosis, method, consent, rights, measurement, research, licensing, governance, and validation. Each supports the method; none substitutes for the others.

## How does the practice work?

The chain is:

```text
Goals → Method → Offering → Evidence and learning → revision of the goals and method
```

The method is:

1. Name one practice, encounter, or information system.
2. Define the boundary and affected people.
3. Establish the necessary consent.
4. Notice what happens in attention and in the body.
5. Read the practice through legibility, permeability, forkability, and provenance.
6. Trace extraction, consent, attribution, and value return.
7. Choose one bounded adaptation.
8. Observe the burden, disagreement, and result.
9. Return the learning where rights and consent permit.

Recording is optional. The first pilot does not require participant recordings.

The walkable form of this method is [the journey](JOURNEY.md): six stations that sequence the kit's instruments in this order.

### Field map

| Field | Question it keeps alive | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Buddhism | What conditions shape attention, and what response may reduce harm? | Self-observation, not diagnosis or proof about a system. |
| Information theory | What moves through a channel, what does it mean, and what can someone do next? | Shannon's technical theory is not a theory of meaning or ethics. |
| Evaluation | What evidence would support, weaken, or contradict the claim? | Current instruments are hypotheses, not validated measures. |
| Somatic practice | What does contact do in the body, including urgency, contraction, fatigue, or ease? | Experience informs a reading; it is not a clinical judgment or objective score. |

### Collaboration swimlane

| Collaborator brings | Together | Isaac brings | What returns |
|---|---|---|---|
| One real practice, its boundary, the affected people, and what must stay private | Scope the question, consent, evidence, and a result that could count against the method | The journey scoped to the practice, the rights and extraction questions, and a public/private boundary review | A public-safe field report when permitted, plus a correction, disagreement, refusal, or method revision |

The collaboration is complete only when the burden and disagreement are recorded and someone affected by the change can contest the reading. It does not require participant footage or a public release of participant material.

### Four accesses and the extraction check

| Access | Plain-language question |
|---|---|
| Legibility | Can someone outside the core understand what exists, what it does, and whether it concerns them? |
| Permeability | Can information, questions, and corrections cross the boundary in both directions? |
| Forkability | Can another person legally and practically adapt the material? |
| Provenance | Can someone trace a claim or artifact to an appropriate source and see how it changed? |

Keep these four readings separate. Pair them with the [extraction check](EXTRACTION-CHECK.md). Missing evidence remains `unmeasurable`; there is no composite score.

### Key definitions

- **Buddhism:** living traditions whose teachings PureLand uses carefully and partially as a prompt for self-observation.
- **Information theory:** the technical study of information transmission and communication; here it helps distinguish movement from meaning and affordance.
- **Evaluation:** disciplined inquiry that names a claim, evidence, baseline, limits, and possible disconfirmation.
- **Somatic practice:** attention to embodied experience as information for reflection, not diagnosis.
- **Legibility:** the ability of an outside person to understand what exists and why it matters.
- **Permeability:** the ability of information, questions, and corrections to cross a boundary.
- **Forkability:** the legal and practical ability to adapt a method without an existing relationship.
- **Provenance:** a traceable account of origin, transformation, and authority.
- **Consent:** permission for a specific use, informed enough to choose, revisable, and withdrawable within stated limits.
- **Attribution:** keeping origin and transformation visible without forcing identification or creating surveillance risk.
- **Value:** what leaves a contributor or participant and accumulates elsewhere, including attention, data, labor, money, status, relationship, or decision power.
- **Return:** what comes back in a form the originator recognizes as meaningful, such as access, credit, a copy, payment, learning, control, or decision power.

## What keeps it accountable?

The [protocol](PROTOCOL.md) separates consent for recording, retention, review, publication, research, and AI use. [RIGHTS-AND-CONSENT.md](RIGHTS-AND-CONSENT.md) distinguishes copyright, privacy, confidentiality, and community authority. The [scorecard](SCORECARD.md) does not certify openness. The extraction check does not produce a moral verdict. [FIELD-TESTING.md](FIELD-TESTING.md) requires missing evidence, burden, disagreement, and disconfirmation to stay visible.

The project is currently maintained by Isaac Rubinstein, who holds merge and release authority. That concentration is a risk recorded in [GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md). The kit's eligible text and templates use [CC BY-SA 4.0](LICENSE), while code and configuration use MIT. A license does not create permission for participant or third-party material.

## What can you do with it now?

The present offering is the [PureLand Field Pilot](OFFERING.md): a bounded co-inquiry into one real information practice. You can also fork the instruments, submit a public-safe field test, document a failure or refusal, or propose a correction through the contribution process.

The future consultancy is an application layer on a 10-to-15-year horizon. PureLand is not presenting itself as a mature consultancy now. The present phase is external criticism and bounded field proof.

## What do we know, and what remains untested?

Known: the kit has a protocol, four-access scorecard, extraction check, provenance record, rights guidance, and governance rules. The closest identified precedent for combining participatory media and evaluation is InsightShare's [Participatory Video and the Most Significant Change](https://insightshare.org/resources/participatory-video-and-the-most-significant-change/). PureLand does not claim to originate that combination.

Untested: the instruments have no independent field trial, reliability estimate, validity result, benchmark, threshold, certification, or outcome estimate. The [field-trial ledger](FIELD-TRIALS.md) is empty. The next research gate is five to ten independent applications across at least three contexts, with two readers where practical. See [RESEARCH-STATUS.md](RESEARCH-STATUS.md).
