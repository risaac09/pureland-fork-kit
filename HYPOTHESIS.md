# The PureLand hypothesis

The [thesis](THESIS.md) describes the shape of the work. The hypothesis makes a claim that field evidence can weaken or defeat.

## Primary hypothesis

> When a person walks the PureLand method on one bounded information practice and tests one contestable adaptation, their practical ability to understand, reach, adapt, trace, stop, or refuse the practice can improve without a material increase in exposure, extraction, or shifted burden.

This is a working hypothesis, not a demonstrated effect.

## Required observations

The result is a profile, not one score:

| Dimension | Evidence to record | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Access | Understandable, reachable, adaptable, and traceable results | Keep all four separate |
| Reciprocity | Consent, attribution, and value-return bands | Keep all three separate |
| Agency | Ability to stop, continue, question, change, or refuse | Name the person and the concrete action |
| Burden | Time, money, attention, exposure, relational cost, and possible new harm | Record who pays |
| Contestability | Disagreement, participant objection, correction route, and review authority | Do not smooth conflict into agreement |
| Return | What came back, to whom, in what form, and under whose permission | No public return without rights and consent |

## Disconfirming results

Any of these results should weaken or defeat the primary hypothesis for the tested context:

- the method produces no useful reading beyond an ordinary review of the same evidence;
- the adaptation increases legibility for the assessor while increasing exposure for an affected person;
- safeguards mainly reduce institutional liability while shifting material burden to participants;
- the person cannot stop, refuse, correct, or challenge the reading in practice;
- missing evidence is converted into a favorable score;
- the return is meaningful to the maintainer but not to the people who generated the value;
- independent readers cannot use the constructs consistently enough to support the stated interpretation;
- the burden of the full journey is not justified by what it adds over a smaller instrument.

## Secondary hypotheses

The current research program also tests whether:

1. the three poisons and five hindrances prompt useful self-observation in information practice;
2. the four access questions distinguish useful properties;
3. separate consent, attribution, and value-return profiles expose overlooked asymmetries;
4. one bounded adaptation can increase control over attention;
5. the journey produces better first-time completion and report quality than a menu of instruments.

Each secondary hypothesis can fail on its own. Evidence for one does not validate the others.

## Current status

The primary hypothesis is untested as an outcome claim. [FT-001](research/field-tests/ft-001-alchemy.md) is a maintainer-side worked example. It tested whether the journey added analytic value, not whether PureLand increased a person's attention sovereignty. See [RESULTS.md](RESULTS.md).
