# PureLand Field Pilot

The present offering is a small, bounded inquiry into one real information practice, done together. It walks [the journey](JOURNEY.md), the kit's one curated path, on the collaborator's practice. It is a field pilot. It is not a mature consultancy, certification, treatment, or audit clearance. A proposal does not imply acceptance, and a pilot does not promise an outcome.

The future consultancy is an application layer on a 10-to-15-year horizon. The present phase is criticism from outside and small field proofs.

## Who the pilot is for

The pilot is for a person or small group who runs, takes part in, or is affected by an information practice. Examples include facilitation, participatory media, evaluation, community archives, organizational knowledge, and AI-enabled workflows. The practice must be specific enough to draw a boundary around. The collaborator must be able to say, in general terms, what can be inspected and what must stay private.

There are no published eligibility rules, certification requirements, or guaranteed outcomes. Isaac may decline or defer a proposal after scoping.

## Authority in the pilot

A collaborator may authorize access only to material and decisions within the collaborator's control. Collaborator authority does not replace:

- each participant's authority over their own material;
- a client's authority over confidential records;
- a community's authority over protected knowledge or community-held decisions; or
- a third party's copyright, privacy, or publicity interests.

When the needed authority is missing or disputed, that part of the inquiry stays private or stops. One participant cannot grant community authority.

## What the collaborator brings

- one real practice, encounter, or information system;
- the boundary and affected roles, described without exposing people;
- evidence that can be reviewed lawfully, if any;
- the doubts, burdens, refusals, and disagreements already visible; and
- a clear account of which decisions the collaborator can make and whose permission is still needed.

Missing evidence stays `unmeasurable`. A collaborator may refuse recording, a method step, public reporting, or the pilot itself without that refusal being treated as failure or noncooperation.

## What Isaac brings

- a bounded frame for the inquiry;
- [the journey](JOURNEY.md), walked in order at the depth the practice needs;
- the consent, rights, credit, and value-return questions;
- a review of what stays public and what stays private;
- a second-reader arrangement where practical; and
- a record of failure, disagreement, refusal, or method revision rather than an endorsement.

## What happens together

1. Name the question, system boundary, affected people, available evidence, and one result that would count against the method.
2. Establish the consent and rights the inquiry needs. Recording is optional.
3. Notice what happens in attention and in the body, using the practice frame when the collaborator chooses it.
4. Read the practice through the four access questions and extraction check.
5. Choose one small change to test, if a safe and authorized change exists, and name a plausible new harm.
6. Watch the cost, disagreement, refusal, and result. Keep missing outcomes `unmeasurable`.
7. Decide whether anything can go public. Private learning may be the complete return.

## What the pilot may produce

Possible outputs include:

- a defined system boundary;
- a consent and rights plan;
- a profile across the four access questions;
- an extraction and reciprocity profile;
- one tested change;
- a documented correction, disagreement, refusal, or method revision; or
- a public-safe field report, only when consent and rights permit the actual report.

No public field report is required. The pilot does not require participant recordings, transcripts, names, film production, or a measurable outcome. No one must publish as compensation, return, proof of reciprocity, or proof that the pilot succeeded.

## What remains private

Participant and client material, consent and relationship records, personal notes, protected community knowledge, credentials, identifying data, confidential records, and third-party material without clear reuse rights stay outside the public kit. The limits on withdrawal must be stated before collection. See [RIGHTS-AND-CONSENT.md](RIGHTS-AND-CONSENT.md).

## What the pilot does not claim

The pilot does not claim validation, causal proof, safety certification, attention sovereignty as an outcome, a moral verdict, a combined openness score, or a general finding from one case. It does not promise pricing, duration, session count, certification, an outcome, or a film deliverable. The commercial structure and other future consultancy details remain unresolved.

## How to propose a pilot

Stage 1 is a public-safe scoping issue. Use the [field-test form](https://github.com/risaac09/pureland-fork-kit/issues/new?template=field-test.yml) to provide:

- the practice in general terms and why the question matters;
- the proposed scope;
- whether participant, client, confidential, community-held, or identifying material exists;
- what categories must remain private; and
- one result that would count against the method.

Do not include protected material or public evidence links that expose it. Public evidence links are not required when participant or protected material may be involved. The issue begins scoping only. It does not accept the pilot or promise work.

Stage 2 begins only after the maintainer establishes an appropriate private review channel and confirms the rights basis. This repository does not name a default private system. Until that channel exists, no participant material should enter the issue, repository, or an AI system. A field test may end in refusal or private learning, with no public report.
