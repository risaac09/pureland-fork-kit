# Extraction check

This is the trace station of [the journey](JOURNEY.md). Use this after the [openness scorecard](SCORECARD.md), or before opening a system further. Apply it to your own practice first.

## 1. Trace value

Value means anything that leaves a person and piles up somewhere else. Include attention, narrative, relationship, data, labor, money, status, and decision power.

## 2. Describe three reciprocities

Rate each dimension with one band. Cite evidence. Do not average or multiply the bands.

| Band | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Absent | No meaningful mechanism or evidence |
| Formal | A stated rule exists, but the affected person has little practical control |
| Usable | The mechanism can be exercised without unreasonable burden |
| Shared | The affected person helps set the terms and receives a meaningful return |
| Unmeasurable | The record needed to judge is missing |

### Consent

Is permission specific, informed, ongoing, and withdrawable within stated technical and legal limits?

The old extraction coefficient has been retired. Multiplying subjective ratings created false precision, assumed the dimensions were equal and independent, and produced thresholds with no validation evidence behind them.

## 3. Check attention sovereignty

- What cue starts the contact?
- Can the person stop without a penalty or a manufactured sense of loss?
- Does the system show its duration, its state, and its consequences?
- Which poison or hindrance may be present in my own use or design?
- What evidence would prove my reading wrong?

The contemplative category is a prompt, not a score.

## 4. Check the sovereignty paradox

Protection can extract too. Record who pays the time, money, mental, and relational cost of each safeguard. Ask whether the safeguard increases the participant's agency or mainly reduces the institution's liability.

## 5. Use a collective lens

One person's consent may not settle the use of a community's knowledge. Ask who has the authority to decide, who benefits, which community protocols apply, and where accountability lives. The [CARE Principles](https://doi.org/10.5334/dsj-2020-043) are one relevant source, not a universal substitute for local governance.
