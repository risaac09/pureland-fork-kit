# The Extraction Check

*(Pure)Land fork kit, component 3 of 5. Run this on your own practice after every [scorecard](SCORECARD.md) reading, and on any system you are about to open further. Openness and extractability rise together unless consent, attribution, and return travel with the material.*

## What this is

A short assessment of where value flows in a practice: who contributes it, where it accumulates, and whether it returns to origin. The point is not to label a practice extractive or regenerative. Everything sits on a gradient. The point is to see the topology honestly and find where to intervene.

Apply it to yourself first. The check was built to be self-applied, and a practice that only audits others has already failed it.

## Step 1: trace the value

Name the forms of value moving through the practice: narrative (stories, meanings, identities), attention, relationship, data, money, time. For each one that matters, answer four questions:

- Where does it originate?
- Where does it accumulate?
- Does it return to origin?
- In what form does it return, and is that form degraded (a story taken, "visibility" returned)?

## Step 2: score the three reciprocities

Score each from 0 to 1. Be stingy; the failure mode of self-assessment is generosity.

**Consent reciprocity (C_r).** Is consent informed, ongoing, and meaningful? Can it be withdrawn without penalty? Does old consent quietly govern new uses it never contemplated?

| Score | Reads as |
|---|---|
| 0.0 to 0.2 | No meaningful consent; take it or leave it |
| 0.3 to 0.4 | Formal consent, uninformed or coerced |
| 0.5 to 0.6 | Informed, but no withdrawal mechanism |
| 0.7 to 0.8 | Ongoing, withdrawal possible |
| 0.9 to 1.0 | Full consent sovereignty with real alternatives |

**Attribution reciprocity (A_r).** Are the people the value came from credited? Does the credit survive transformation and republication, or does it sever the first time the material moves?

| Score | Reads as |
|---|---|
| 0.0 to 0.2 | No attribution; value anonymized |
| 0.3 to 0.4 | Attribution exists, easily severed |
| 0.5 to 0.6 | Persistent but unenforced |
| 0.7 to 0.8 | Attribution tied to the value flow |
| 0.9 to 1.0 | Full provenance tracking |

**Value reciprocity (V_r).** Do originators receive proportional return, in comparable form? Or does the practice keep the durable asset and return exposure?

| Score | Reads as |
|---|---|
| 0.0 to 0.2 | No return to originators |
| 0.3 to 0.4 | Token return (exposure, visibility) |
| 0.5 to 0.6 | Partial return, degraded form |
| 0.7 to 0.8 | Substantial return, approaching equity |
| 0.9 to 1.0 | Originators are the primary beneficiaries |

**Extraction coefficient:**

E_c = 1 / (C_r x A_r x V_r)

| E_c | Tendency |
|---|---|
| under 1.5 | Regenerative |
| 1.5 to 3.0 | Tension |
| 3.0 to 10.0 | Extractive |
| over 10.0 | Highly extractive |

If you cannot score all three because the record does not exist, the coefficient is **unmeasurable**, and that is the finding: the practice keeps no record of consent, attribution, and return. Write that down instead of a number. Do not invent subscores to get a coefficient.

## Step 3: the sovereignty paradox

Protection can extract too. Check who bears the cost of your safeguards:

- Does the consent process take more time from the people with the least of it?
- Does friction protect the participant, or mostly protect you?
- Are community bonds being leveraged for compliance ("everyone else signed")?

High-friction governance that loads its cost onto resource-poor participants is its own form of taking. If your safeguards are heavy, name who is paying for them and what support (time, money, an intermediary) would rebalance it.

## Step 4: the collective lens

Where material concerns a community rather than an individual, individual consent is not the whole question. The CARE principles for Indigenous data governance name the standard: collective benefit, authority to control, responsibility, ethics. Ask the four directly: does the community benefit, does it control collection and use, is the accountability relational rather than merely legal, are its protocols respected? A practice can pass every individual-consent test and still fail all four of these.

## Self-check before you file the result

- [ ] Did I trace value flow, not just list stakeholders?
- [ ] Did I score C_r, A_r, V_r explicitly, or honestly write "unmeasurable"?
- [ ] Did I check my own safeguards for the sovereignty paradox?
- [ ] Did I apply the collective lens where the material is collective?
- [ ] Did I apply all of this to my own practice, not only to the system I was examining?

A high E_c does not make a practice evil. It marks where to intervene: raise the weakest reciprocity first, and re-run the check after the intervention, not instead of it.
