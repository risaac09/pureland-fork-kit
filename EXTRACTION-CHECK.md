## 2. Describe three reciprocities

Use one band for each dimension. Cite evidence. Do not average or multiply the bands.

| Band | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Absent | No meaningful mechanism or evidence |
| Formal | A stated rule exists, but the affected person has little practical control |
| Usable | The mechanism can be exercised without unreasonable burden |
| Shared | The affected person helps govern the terms and receives meaningful return |
| Unmeasurable | The record needed to judge is missing |

## 3. Check attention sovereignty

- What cue initiates contact?
- Can the person stop without penalty or manufactured loss?
- Does the system expose duration, state, and consequences?
- Which poison or hindrance may be present in my own use or design?
- What evidence would disconfirm my reading?

The contemplative category is a prompt, not a score.

## 4. Check the sovereignty paradox

Protection can extract. Record who pays the time, money, cognitive, and relational cost of each safeguard. Ask whether the safeguard increases the participant's agency or mainly reduces institutional liability.

## 5. Use a collective lens

Individual consent may not settle the use of collective knowledge. Ask who has authority to decide, who benefits, which community protocols apply, and where accountability lives. The CARE Principles are one relevant source, not a universal substitute for local governance.
